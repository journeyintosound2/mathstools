The Unit Circle — Trigonometric Ratios of Any Angle — Worksheet Generator

Printable A4 worksheets from The Unit Circle teaching tool and its flip cards. On the unit circle the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ), so the sign of each ratio is the sign of a coordinate, and every angle has a related angle in the first quadrant with the same size of answer. The paper climbs from reading the circle, through signs, related angles and exact values, to solving equations, reading the graphs and the tool’s own applications: the Ferris wheel, the tide, the pendulum and the hours of daylight.

The angles — tick one level, or several for a ladder

Quick sets:

One level ticked: every question is that level. Several ticked: each section climbs through them, easiest first. Sweet: the second quadrant, 30°, 45° and 60°. Mild: second and third quadrants, multiples of 10°. Medium: all four quadrants, negative answers to equations. Spicy: angles past 360° and below 0°, two-step equations and calculator answers to the nearest degree.

The ratios and how they are written

sin and cos only is a good first paper; tan is y ÷ x and comes next. On the printed circles the orange wedge is the related angle and the faint triangle shows the same angle in the first quadrant, as in the teaching tool.

Scaffolding and the paper

Unit circles to draw on are switched on in C, D and F above (one circle per part, or one per equation in F): students draw the stick at the angle and drop the triangle to find the related angle. The answer key draws the working in. Ticks help weaker students place the angle.

The worked example draws sin 210° on the unit circle, finds the related angle and the sign, then solves sin θ = −½. The answer key gives every answer with a one-line reason, so it can be handed to a student.
