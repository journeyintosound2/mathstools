Number Groups — Collecting Like Terms — Worksheet Generator

Printable A4 worksheets from the Number Groups teaching tool (and its Student Quiz). A term is some groups of the same thing, so collecting like terms is counting the groups first: 3 × 7 + 2 × 7 + 5 × 7 = 10 × 7, and the same picture with a letter, x + 3x + 6x = 10x. The bars are printed in the first sections, students draw their own in the third, and from then on the picture is in their heads.

The numbers — tick one level, or several for a ladder

Quick sets:

One level ticked: every question is that size. Several ticked: each section climbs through them, easiest first. Sweet: two or three terms, all added. Mild: a single group (x) and groups taken away. Medium: two-letter groups (ab), big numbers. Spicy: two kinds of term at once, and answers that cancel or go below zero.

How the terms are written

Words writes the first sections as 3 groups of 7 and 3 groups of x — the meaning, before the notation. The count first line under each question in D–G asks how many groups before the answer; switch it off when the class no longer needs it.

The order is the lesson, and the picture fades: count the groups with the bars printed (numbers, then the same picture with a letter), draw the groups yourself, then collect, take away, missing terms, unlike terms, count first then work it out, spot the mistake and make your own. K Fluency is off until you tick it: bare questions, good as a warm-up page on its own.

Scaffolding and the paper

The worked example draws 3 × 7 + 2 × 7 + 5 × 7 as bars stacked into one pile of 10 groups, then the same pile with x. The answer key gives every answer with a one-line reason (how many groups of what), so it can be handed to a student.
