Area Model — Multiplication & Perimeter — Worksheet Generator

Printable A4 worksheets from the Area Model — Multiplication & Perimeter teaching tool. Split a rectangle into parts that are easy to multiply and add the pieces for the area; the same splits break each edge into parts to add for the perimeter.

What are we practising?

Area keeps the multiplication sections (A–C), and the cut-out shapes and fluency ask for area. Perimeter keeps the addition sections (D–E), and they ask for perimeter. Both keeps everything.

The numbers — tick one level, or several for a ladder

Quick sets:

One level ticked: every question is that size. Several ticked: each section climbs through them, easiest first.

The model

Fade out draws the splits for the first half of each section and leaves the rest blank for students to split themselves. Perimeter working sets the scaffold lines in section D: tens and ones, double the half, both in turn, or an open working box. A mix of splits includes non-standard ones such as 23 = 15 + 8 or 14 = 7 + 7. Unit squares print when the rectangle is small enough to draw to scale with squares you can count (most Stage 3 sizes); bigger rectangles print as open area models.

The order is the lesson: multiply with the model, see that two splits give one answer, work backwards, then add the edges, make a ten, and cut pieces out. G Fluency is off until you tick it: bare questions with no model, good as a warm-up page on its own.

The paper

Write the multiplication in each piece prints a small “20 × 10” at the top of every drawn piece, leaving room underneath for the product — turn it off once students can read the pieces for themselves. The answer key fills in every piece, side and step, so it can be handed to a student. Looking for the ready-made sheet? Matchstick Troubles (PDF).
