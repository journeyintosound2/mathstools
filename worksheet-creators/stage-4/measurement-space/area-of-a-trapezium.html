Area of a Trapezium — Worksheet Generator

Printable A4 worksheets from the Area of a Trapezium teaching tool (and its Student Quiz). Three ways to see the area, drawn on the paper: swing the corners round into a rectangle as long as the average of the parallel sides, join two copies into a parallelogram and halve it, or halve the height — cut it across halfway up and turn the top half round. Then find areas, work backwards, spot the mistake and draw your own.

The numbers — tick one level, or several for a ladder

Quick sets:

One level ticked: every question is that size. Several ticked: each section climbs through them, easiest first. Sweet keeps the average of the parallel sides a whole number; from Medium some areas end in a half (22.5). Working backwards always comes out to whole numbers.

The trapeziums

The kinds you tick are mixed through every section (at least one stays on). A sloping side is labelled with a whole-number length (3-4-5, 6-8-10, 5-12-13 …) so students must choose the height — the length at right angles to the parallel sides. Turned trapeziums have their parallel sides running up the page, so the height runs across (sections D, E and F only). Unit squares print only on upright trapeziums with whole-number lengths, when the squares are big enough to count.

The order is the lesson: the three ways to see it (average base, half a parallelogram, halve the height), find areas, work backwards, spot the mistake, then draw your own. H Fluency is off until you tick it: bare questions with no diagram, good as a warm-up page on its own.

Scaffolding and the paper

Turn the formula off when the class is still discovering it — the example then shows the swing, the two copies or the turned top half, without ½ × (a + b) × h. The answer key fills in every answer with a one-line reason, so it can be handed to a student.
