Area of a Triangle — Worksheet Generator

Printable A4 worksheets from the Area of a Triangle — Half the Rectangle teaching tool. Every triangle is half of a base × height rectangle — the sheets start there, then find areas, choose the right height, work backwards and reason about why.

The numbers — tick one level, or several for a ladder

Quick sets:

One level ticked: every question is that size. Several ticked: each section climbs through them, easiest first. Medium and Spicy include odd base × height, so some areas are halves (22.5).

The triangles

Untick a kind to leave it out (at least one stays on). A sloping side with a whole-number length (3-4-5, 6-8-10, 5-12-13 …) is labelled as well as the base and height, so students have to pick the length at right angles to the base. Turned triangles sit on any side — the base is no longer at the bottom, so students find it from the right angle. Unit squares print only on triangles that are not turned and are small enough to count.

On the paper:0 questions

The order is the lesson: see the rectangle it is half of, find areas, notice that same base, same height means same area, work backwards, spot the mistake, then draw your own. G Fluency is off until you tick it: bare questions with no diagram, good as a warm-up page on its own.

Scaffolding and the paper

Fade out gives the first half of section B the frame and the rest plain working lines. Turn the formula off when the class is still discovering why a triangle is half a rectangle — the example then shows the rectangle and “half of it” only. The answer key fills in every answer with a one-line reason, so it can be handed to a student.
