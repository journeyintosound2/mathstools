Fraction of an Amount — Worksheet Generator

Printable A4 worksheets from the Fraction of an Amount — Divide, Then Multiply teaching tool. A bar holding the amount is cut into equal parts, so one part is a division: 15 of 20 is 20 ÷ 5. More than one part is that division done again: 25 of 20 = 2 × (20 ÷ 5). The paper takes the bar away one step at a time until the notation carries the idea on its own.

Quick sets:

The level sets the size of one part (the division a student does), so Sweet and Mild keep that division inside the tables they know. One level ticked: every question is that size. Several ticked: each section climbs through them, easiest first.

The fractions and the bars

Halves, quarters, fifths and tenths are the fractions the Stage 3 syllabus names for fractions of a quantity (MA3-RQF-02). Every amount shares into whole parts, and random fractions are always in simplest form. Colours match the teaching tool: the amount blue, the number of parts purple, the parts taken orange.

The order is the lesson: one part is a division, more parts is divide then multiply, students draw the bar themselves, then the bar goes and the notation carries it — four ways to write it, true or false, a mistake to fix, word problems, and building the whole back from a part. I Fluency is off until you tick it: bare questions in all four forms, good as a warm-up page on its own.

Scaffolding and the paper

Turn the rule off while the class is still noticing it — the example then shows the bar and its two steps without “divide by the bottom number, multiply by the top”. The answer key fills in every bar and gap, with a one-line reason where it helps.
