# Emerald Jungle — ten proportional-reasoning challenges

Status (2026-10-10, evening): **all ten challenges are built** — the shared Vine Ladder, Monkey Swap, Sunny Market (both reworked), Lagoon Race, Tall Tree Tape, Mushroom Potions, Waterfall Barrels, Treasure Map, Firefly Graphs, Temple of Truth and Pathfinder, with the jungle trophy stand + grid, the Achievements Wall board, the map pins and Mills' welcome. **Not yet deployed.** The land is described in `claude/emerald-jungle-world.md`; the build follows the Magma Multiples pattern (registry, stage frames, hosts, 15-round sets, local bests, trophy stand).

Jeff's brief: the next phase after additive (Snowball Sums) and multiplicative (Magma Multiples) thinking is **proportional reasoning**, built around the **double number line** in different contexts — unit conversions, speed/distance/time, rates, "if x of these is worth y of these" — where students work out values by scaling (double, halve, add, …). Students interact with the proportional relationship rather than just answer questions. Trophy stand + a place on the Achievements Wall.

---

## 1. The rule that shapes everything: reward accuracy, not speed (Jeff, 2026-10-10)

> "The idea is to develop proportional thinking, not necessarily to get the answer the fastest … the person that does it in 2 would receive the same XP as the person that does it in 4 so long as the person that did it in 4 made no mistakes. The teacher can explain the unitary method later but not here."

So, in every jungle challenge:
- **Any route up the ladder scores the same.** There is no efficiency bonus, no "fewest moves", no "it can be done in 2" on the slip card, and no "quick way" shown. The feedback card says "here's one way to see it" and shows one route, never "the" route.
- **Only wrong numbers cost.** A round is worth **25 XP**; each wrong number (a wrong partner, a wrong typed answer, a wrong non-final call such as "same potion?" or a wrong Temple reason) costs **4**; a round whose final answer is right never earns less than **9**. If the FINAL call is wrong (best buy, who's faster, which is bluer…) the work before it keeps **10 − 4 per wrong number**; a wrong first call (the Temple's works/breaks, matching a firefly story) ends the round with 0.
- **Refusals are not mistakes.** Asking for half a banana, part of a cent, a rung off the end of the vine or a rung that's already there is refused with a nudge and costs nothing.
- **Nobody is steered to "go through one".** The unitary method is available (Split…, then Times…) and a student may discover it, but no intro, hint, wording or score pushes it. Up to 14 new rungs per ladder, so long routes are never cut off.
- Set = 15 rounds (5 stages × 3) = 375 max; 🥇 100%, 🥈 75%, 🥉 50%.
- A check (JCF6) proves it for all ten: a round played with an extra correct detour still earns 25, and one wrong number earns 21.

## 2. One tool, ten places — the Vine Ladder

Every challenge uses the **same** double number line, dressed for its context, so students meet the same structure ten times (Gravemeijer's emergent modelling; ICCAMS: let the DNL emerge over time).

- Two vines tied together at a carved **0 post** ("tied at zero"), each labelled with ONE quantity and unit. A known pair is a **bamboo rung**.
- **Moves:** again (another copy of the starting rung), × 2, ÷ 2, × 10, ÷ 10, Split… (into n equal pieces on both vines), Times…, Join two, Gap, and on the tape **↔ Across**. Tap a rung (or two) or use the card's strips; the scene fills in one end and **the student types the other end**.
- **What wrong looks like:** a wrong partner hangs a **crooked rung**; adding the same to both **unties the zero** (the bottom vine slides, "0 ← slid!"); then each context adds its own consequence (the racer swims past your flag, the barrel overflows, the explorer digs and finds nothing, the potion turns the wrong colour, the firefly sits off the line).
- **Layouts:** one ladder · a three-vine "shared" ladder (two ladders sharing a middle vine, so a common amount lines up straight down — best buys, races, potions, two pipes, three-edged tapes) · chained ladders (Barrels S3, Pathfinder expeditions) · a graph (Firefly Graphs stands the ladder up).

Hosts reuse the staff glbs (Jeff's decision). Each stage stands beside its clearing's trail — never on it — so you walk round the apparatus, not through it.

### 1 · Monkey Swap — Palm Grove (Mr. Morgan)
A ratio is a composed unit you can iterate (Lamon; Lobato & Ellis). Fruit hangs on the vines in the early stages (3 bananas above every 2 mangoes — the bundle you can see), then fades so the numbers do the work.
S1 build it up (again only) · S2 jump ahead (× 2, × 10, join; up to 40 swaps) · S3 backwards (the mangoes lead) · S4 half a swap (even deals; halving helps) · S5 the smallest swap (12 ↔ 8 split down until it won't split into whole fruit, then claim it — an early claim is a slip).

### 2 · Sunny Market — Sunny Knoll (Mr. Pearce) — reworked for the accuracy rule
Fruit on top, dollars below (whole cents only). **Reframed:** stage 1 was "go through one"; now it is "any way up" — halve and join, double, or find one lemon; all equal.
S1 any way up ("4 lemons cost $9. How much for 7?") · S2 friendly pieces (one papaya isn't whole cents, so a piece of 3 or 2 is friendlier — the refusal nudges, it doesn't cost) · S3 how many can I buy? (the dollar vine leads) · S4 best buy (a three-vine ladder: A's $ · fruit · B's $; build both to the same fruit, then choose; the stall with more fruit always costs more in total — the "bigger looks cheaper" trap) · S5 best buy by the kilo.

### 3 · Lagoon Race — Lily Lagoon (Mr. Heywood)
Seconds on the top vine, metres below; frog, turtle and duck race in lanes in front of the ladder. A wrong distance at the target is a slip — the racer swims past your flag.
S1 how far? · S2 how long? (metres lead) · S3 who's faster? (shared SECONDS vine — build both to the same time; trap: the bigger distance on the card is the slower racer) · S4 race to the bank (shared METRES vine — who gets there first; trap: the winner has the bigger time number) · S5 kayak tour (km and hours with tenths: how far, how long in whole/half hours, and how far in ONE hour — its speed).

### 4 · Tall Tree Tape — The Great Tree (Ms. Ewings)
The "between the lines" multiplier (Vergnaud's function relation; ICCAMS 91% along vs 51% across) gets a whole challenge. The tape hangs vertically from a bamboo gantry; a monkey climbs it. Students first **spot the across number** on three given rungs (typed — a wrong one is a slip), which unlocks **↔ Across** — or they keep building along. Both are equally good.
S1 metric pairs (m ↔ cm, km ↔ m, L ↔ mL, cm ↔ mm) · S2 kitchen measures (cups × 250 mL, tbsp × 20, tsp × 5) · S3 paces ("8 paces ≈ 6 m" — along only: working along always works) · S4 three edges (m | cm | mm or h | min | s, with the across move free) · S5 along or across (kg ↔ g, h ↔ min, $ ↔ cents, L ↔ mL). The third round of each stage runs backwards (650 cm → ? m — across means ÷). **Metric only.**

### 5 · Mushroom Potions — Mushroom Glade (Ms. Brookes)
Moonberry 🔵 on top, sunfruit 🟡 below; the cauldron takes the colour of what's poured (a real pigment mix), next to a vial of the recipe colour and a "too blue · just right · too yellow" meter so colour is never the only cue.
S1 bigger batch · S2 same potion? (Fern scaled it × 3, or added 2 to both — call SAME or DIFFERENT, a wrong call is a slip, then fix it on the ladder) · S3 which is bluer? (shared BLUE vine; Noelting's task; trap: more blue scoops isn't bluer) · S4 fill the cauldron (part : part : whole — the shared middle vine is the TOTAL) · S5 three ingredients (a red ember-berry, 2 : 3 : 1).

### 6 · Waterfall Barrels — Waterfall Pool (Trevor)
Seconds on top, litres below; a bamboo pipe fills a barrel. A wrong time over- or under-fills it.
S1 how much in a minute? · S2 fill it (the litres lead; type the seconds) · S3 per minute, per hour (two ladders in a row: litres in 1 minute, then a MINUTES ladder to 1 hour) · S4 two pipes (shared seconds vine, then the total they pour together) · S5 rates everywhere (heartbeats per minute, drumbeats, ants carrying leaves, rain in mm per hour).

### 7 · Treasure Map — Canopy Lookout (Ms. Mahoney)
Map cm on the top vine (on a strip of parchment with the dotted route and the X), real metres below — the bottom vine IS the trail: the explorer walks it and digs where the student says.
S1 simple scale (1 cm = 10 m) · S2 scale bars (2 cm = 50 m) · S3 backwards (the real vine leads) · S4 ratio scales (1 : 2500 — first type what 1 cm is in metres; the ladder stays hidden until then so the answer isn't given away) · S5 long routes (1 : 25 000, two legs, then the whole trip).

### 8 · Firefly Graphs — Redwood Hollow (Mr. Dawson)
Stand the ladder up: minutes along the ground, km up a redwood. Every rung is a firefly; a true ladder's fireflies sit on ONE straight line from the roots. A wrong number is a firefly off the line; adding the same to both makes a line that misses the roots.
S1 light the line · S2 steeper is faster (two walkers: hang both at the same time, then call the steeper) · S3 read the line (the line is lit; distance leads) · S4 match the story (three lines, each with one firefly at a different time; which matches "3 km every 45 min"? hang more fireflies on any line to check — the call is the round) · S5 speed on the line (how far in ONE hour, then how long for a longer walk).

### 9 · Temple of Truth — Temple Ruins (Robot)
The ONE place non-proportional situations appear — as the announced topic, never a trick (Jeff: no trick rounds elsewhere). Each tablet shows a jungle situation and three pairs. Call "the ladder works" or "the ladder breaks" (a wrong call ends the round). Works → finish the missing value (any route). Breaks → say why: **starts with a fee · same difference · stays the same · grows faster and faster · one goes up, the other goes down** (a wrong reason is a slip). **No °C/°F tablet** (°F is imperial).
S1 one obvious each way · S2 starts with a fee · S3 same difference · S4 stays the same / grows faster · one up, one down · S5 mixed. Every stage has at least one tablet that works and one that breaks, shuffled; the set's first tablet always works.

### 10 · Pathfinder — Sky Shrine (Ms. Bacon) — redesigned
The original "three routes — pick the easiest, then do it" capstone rewarded the fastest route, which Jeff's rule rules out. **Now: expeditions that need one ladder after another** — the answer to leg 1 is the target of leg 2. Stepping stones light up leg by leg; a finished expedition lights the shrine's crystal.
S1 one ladder, bigger numbers (swaps · prices · speeds · flow) · S2 one ladder, decimals (kitchen measures · map scales · km/h) · S3 map → walk (map cm → real m → seconds at walking pace) · S4 swap → buy (bananas → mangoes → dollars) · S5 paddle → drink (km → hours → litres of water).

## 5. How it is built
- `src/data/jungle/jungleChallenges.js` — registry: keys, hosts, clearings, stage frames (`clearingFrame(spot, camDeg, back)`), camera views (incl. tall views for the tape and graph), solid footprints, view corridors.
- `src/data/jungle/vineLadder.js` — the shared maths: moves on whole base units (fruit, cents, grams, tenths…), refusals, the add-the-same partner + untied zero, parsing/formatting, the accuracy scoring.
- `src/data/jungle/roundKit.js` — `planRound` (a worked route for each phase — used for the "one way to see it" working and by the checks, never for scoring).
- `src/data/jungle/<key>Challenge.js` — pure round logic, one file per challenge; `jungleUnits.js` the shared vines (seconds, metres, km, L, mL, cups, $, kg, beats…).
- `src/game/jungle/ladderStore.js` — one store factory for all ten (phases: build · common · call · type · smallest); `stores/*` hold each challenge's kid wording.
- 3D: `stages/VineLadder.jsx` + `LadderSet.jsx` (all layouts) and one stage per challenge (`RaceStage`, `TapeStage`, `PotionStage`, `BarrelsStage`, `MapStage`, `FirefliesStage`, `TempleStage`, `PathfinderStage`, plus Swap/Market).
- Card: `ui/jungle/LadderPlay.jsx` (strips, moves, picker, partner/type boxes, calls, explore toggle, leg chips) inside Magma's panel shell; intros in `jungleIntros.jsx`.
- Checks `src/dev/jungleChallengeChecks.js`: JCF1–JCF6 + three per challenge (200-set fuzz, perfect play = 375, the traps).

## 6. Decisions (Jeff, 2026-10-10)
1. Pilot first (done), then the other eight (done).
2. **Metric only** — no imperial units anywhere (so no °F tablet in the Temple).
3. **No trick rounds** outside the Temple of Truth.
4. **Reuse the staff glbs** as hosts.
5. **Reward accuracy, not speed** — any route scores the same; XP is lost only for mistakes; the unitary method is left for the teacher to name later. Sunny Market and Monkey Swap were reworked to this rule, and Pathfinder was redesigned around it.

## 7. Research spine
- NSW Mathematics K–10 (2022) Stage 4 **Ratios and rates, MA4-RAT-C-01** — unitary method, divide in a ratio, ratios vs rates, convert units for rates, best buys, distance–time graphs.
- Küchemann, Hodgen & Brown (2014), ICCAMS: along vs between the lines (91% vs 51%), label zeros, one measure per line, let the model emerge.
- Lamon (2012): unitising, partitioning, relative thinking, proportional vs non-proportional.
- Lobato & Ellis (2010), NCTM Essential Understanding: iterating and partitioning composed units; covariation.
- Vergnaud (1983, 1988): scalar and function relationships.
- Noelting (1980): orange-juice mixture tasks.
- Hart (1981, CSMS); Karplus, Pulos & Stage (1983): the additive strategy.
- De Bock, Van Dooren, Janssens & Verschaffel (2007), *The Illusion of Linearity*; Van Dooren et al. (2009).
- Hilton, Hilton, Dole & Goos (2013, 2016): proportional reasoning across the curriculum.
- Gravemeijer (1999): emergent models; Fosnot & Dolk: the DNL for rates and percents.
- Siemon, Bleckly & Neal (2012): proportional reasoning follows multiplicative thinking.
